Contexto & Setor

The project involved the complete redesign of IVR (Interactive Voice Response) systems — the well-known "voice call centers" — for three banking institutions. IVRs are critical systems: they're the first point of contact for millions of customers with the bank, and their design directly impacts operational-cost metrics, satisfaction, and problem resolution.

The Voice UX challenge is unique: no visual feedback, no way to "scan" the interface, no second chance to re-read something confusing. User cognition operates in a linear mode, heavily dependent on working memory — a radically different context from visual interface design.

"Voice UX isn't about voice. It's about memory. It's about designing for the human mind at its most overloaded: on the phone, likely worried, wanting to resolve something quickly."

O Diagnosis

The initial audit revealed three recurring failure patterns:

Problema 1 Overly long menus: menus with 7–9 options violated working-memory limits (Miller's principle: 7 ± 2). Users forgot the first options by the time they heard the last.
Solution 1 Restructured to a maximum of 4 options per level, with hierarchy based on call-frequency analysis and the GOMS model.
Problema 2 Overly technical, formal language: terms like "liability renegotiation" instead of "renegotiate debt" created confusion and increased navigation errors.
Solution 2 Complete rewrite of the UX Writing scripts using Plain Language principles and comprehension testing with real users before implementation.
Problema 3 Lack of progress feedback: users didn't know what part of the journey they were in — generating anxiety and repeated calls to confirm information.
Solution 3 We introduced contextual confirmations and progress summaries: "Got it. You selected X. Now let's move to Y." — adapting the breadcrumb concept to a voice context.

Metodologia Aplicada

Modelo Cognitivo GOMS

O modelo GOMS (Goals, Operators, Methods, Selection Rules) was applied to map each user task into its minimal cognitive units — identifying where the system demanded more mental effort than necessary. This analysis revealed that 40% of interaction time was spent on orientation actions (trying to figure out where they were), not resolution actions.

Service Blueprinting

Beyond the IVR itself, we ran a complete Service Blueprint of the banking journey — mapping every channel and touchpoint, identifying where the IVR connected (or disconnected) with human support, the app, and internet banking. This revealed hidden redundancies and breakdowns in the customer journey.

Research with Real Users

We conducted voice usability tests with 24 participants across 3 distinct demographic profiles (contrary to expectations: the profile most affected by the issues was users over 55, not younger ones). Tests were recorded and analyzed with a focus on navigation errors and comprehension.

A Nova Arquitetura of Voice

Data-Driven Hierarchy

In partnership with the institutions' data teams, we analyzed the 1,000 most frequent call reasons from the past 12 months. The new menu structure was built on real frequency — not the internal organization of departments (a classic company-centered, rather than user-centered, design mistake).

UX Writing for Voice

Desenvolvemos um guia de Voice UX Writing with specific principles: short sentences (max. 15 words), verb before object ("To pay your bill, press 1" — not "Press 1 to pay your bill"), and confirmations that close open cognitive loops.

Stage 01 · Entry & Main Menu

Maximum 4 options per level

The first screen of the voice flow presents only the 4 most in-demand categories, ordered by call frequency. No long welcome message — the user already knows why they called.

Miller's Law applied 7 ± 2 options is the limit of working memory. We cap it at 4 — the user decides before forgetting the first one.
Frequency-based hierarchy Option 1 = most common reason for calling, not the bank's internal organization.

Stage 02 · Simplified Authentication

Autenticar sem repeating what was already said

Authentication was invisibly built into the flow: a session token follows the user from the IVR to the human agent. When transferred, the customer doesn't need to authenticate again.

Contexto persistente CRM integrated with the IVR: the agent receives the full call history before even saying "hello."
Single-step authentication Reduced from 3 fields to 1 — national ID or voice biometrics, no additional security code.
Stage 03 · Submenu Navigation

Breadcrumb por voz

When entering any submenu, the system confirms the path taken: "You're in Cards > Statement." This orientation feedback, uncommon in IVR systems, reduced navigation errors caused by disorientation by 40%.

GOMS Model — orientation 40% of the time on the old IVR was spent navigating. The verbal breadcrumb eliminates this cognitive load.
"Back" always available "Press star to go back to the previous menu" — present in every submenu, no need to hang up.

Stage 04 · Resolution & Confirmation

Loop cognitivo fechado with confirmation

Every completed transaction is confirmed with a verbal summary and the option to receive a receipt by SMS or email. The user hangs up with certainty — not wondering whether what they asked for actually happened.

Fechamento cognitivo "Your payment of R$XX has been scheduled for XX/XX" — a complete sentence closes the attention loop opened by the reason for the call.
Comprovante multicanal SMS or email offered at the end — eliminates next-day confirmation calls.
